Edition 004 · Paytm · August 2026

Know before you choose.

In April 2026, the RBI cancelled the banking licence of Paytm Payments Bank outright — an entire regulated entity, gone. Paytm's personal loans come from separate NBFC partners, legally unaffected. But it's now a real, visible example of exactly the question this edition investigates: when you borrow through an app, do you actually know who you're borrowing from — and what happens if they leave?

01 · The thesis

Disclosed, but late. Undocumented, everywhere.

This is not a story about hidden fees or secret lenders. Paytm names its lending partner in the app, on the loan agreement, and in the repayment passbook. The real gap is structural, and it's not unique to Paytm: none of the three platforms compared here — Paytm, PhonePe, MobiKwik — publish what happens to your loan if the lender behind it exits. That question stopped being hypothetical the day RBI shut down an entire bank tied to this ecosystem.

01

A bank disappeared, for real

RBI cancelled Paytm Payments Bank's licence outright on 24 April 2026 — the first time it has revoked a payments bank licence in India. One97 (Paytm's parent) says it has no exposure; the personal-loan NBFC partners are separate entities.

02

The lender's name shows up — on screen 4 of 8

Across Paytm's own documented loan journey, the lending partner isn't named at entry, eligibility, or amount selection — only once the offer is generated, in smaller text than Paytm's own branding.

03

This already happened once

In Feb–May 2024, several of Paytm's lending partners paused or exited amid regulatory pressure on Paytm Payments Bank. Existing borrowers kept paying under their original contracts — but no platform published a plan for it in advance.

04

The evidence doesn't support "confused users"

We looked for user complaints about not knowing their lender's identity. We didn't find a documented pattern — see what we rejected.

The information exists. What doesn't exist, on any of the three platforms, is a plan for the day it stops being enough.

Screen-by-screen

The lender's name arrives after the decision

Source: Paytm's own docs

Entry, eligibility, and amount/tenure selection are Paytm-branded only. The lending partner's name first appears on the offer screen — after the user has already chosen how much to borrow — in secondary, smaller text than Paytm's own headings.

Total cost of borrowing

Platform APR ranges compared

Paytm and PhonePe route to lenders typically pricing 9-18% APR. MobiKwik's disclosed range is 17.99-36%. Rates are set entirely by the underlying lender, not the app — which is exactly why "who you're borrowing from" changes the real cost, not just the interface.

03 · What actually happened to the bank

Verified against RBI's own press release, not just headlines.

Confirmed directly on RBI's website (Press Release 2026-2027/143):

01

Licence cancelled outright, 24 Apr 2026

Under Section 22(4) of the Banking Regulation Act, effective close of business that day — the first time RBI has cancelled a payments bank's licence entirely, not just restricted it.

02

RBI's own stated reasons, verbatim

"The affairs of the bank were conducted in a manner detrimental to the interest of the bank and its depositors," and management was "prejudicial to the interest of depositors as also the public interest." RBI's language is general statutory terms, not an itemised list of operational failures.

03

Paytm's separate businesses, by its own filings

One97 Communications states it has "no exposure to PPBL or any material business arrangements with PPBL" and that its investment in PPBL was fully written off by March 2024. The personal-loan NBFC partners are a different, unaffected business line.

04

Deposits are protected; a precedent now exists

RBI confirmed PPBL has enough liquidity to repay all depositors during winding-up. This is the closest real-world case study available for what "an entity behind an app disappears" actually looks like procedurally — worth studying even though it's a different entity from the loan partners.

Evidence-discipline note

The PPBL licence cancellation does not, on the evidence available, directly affect Paytm's personal-loan business. We use it here as a real illustration of institutional fragility, not as a claim about loan safety.

04 · Preserve the value

The lender-disclosure system isn't broken. It's incomplete.

This edition's own contradiction search (Track G) found no documented pattern of user confusion. Whatever gets fixed here should build on real strengths, not manufacture a crisis.

01

The lender's name is genuinely disclosed

On the offer screen, the loan agreement, and the repayment passbook — Paytm's own FAQ states this plainly, and it checks out against the documented screens.

02

Grievance routing is real and multi-level

Paytm, PhonePe and MobiKwik all provide an escalation path from in-app support to a nodal/resolution team to the RBI Ombudsman after 30 days — a genuine industry strength, not just Paytm's.

03

Existing loans survived the 2024 pause

When partners paused lending in 2024, no borrower's existing loan was cancelled or altered — contracts held, under RBI's transfer-of-exposure rules.

04

Fully paperless, fast disbursal

Aadhaar-based eKYC and instant disbursal remain a genuinely strong, low-friction borrowing experience relative to traditional lending.

05 · Opportunity portfolio

Four experiments, aimed at timing and continuity — not a trust crisis.

Because the disconfirming evidence is real, these are framed as improvements to a working system, not fixes to a broken one.

01
High confidence

Know Before You Choose

Move the lending partner's name to the amount/tenure screen — before the offer is generated, not after. The user should see who they're borrowing from at the same moment they decide how much.

Test firstRecall rate: can users correctly name their lender immediately after completing the flow, with vs without early disclosure?
Near termDecision timing
02
High confidence

Partner-Exit Playbook

Publish a plain-language page — for every platform, not just Paytm — explaining what happens to an existing loan if the lending partner exits, using the 2024 precedent as the worked example.

Test firstWhether publishing this proactively reduces support-ticket volume during any future partner transition.
FoundationalStructural gap, industry-wide
03
Medium confidence

Multi-Offer Cost Comparison

Where more than one partner can serve a borrower, show side-by-side APR and fees before auto-assigning one lender — closer to PhonePe's partner-mode flow than Paytm's current single-offer default.

Test firstWhether visible competition among partner offers changes accepted APR versus the current single pre-selected offer.
StrategicCost transparency
04
Medium-high

Granular Consent, Industry-Wide

Replace the single blanket "I agree" with itemised consent for what specifically gets shared with the lending partner and for how long — a gap shared by all three platforms compared here, not a Paytm-specific failure.

Test firstWhether granular consent screens change completion rate meaningfully, or whether friction is acceptable for the trust gained.
Industry-wideConsent design

07 · Action plan

Move disclosure earlier. Document what happens next.

The two highest-confidence fixes don't require new data collection — they require reordering and publishing what already exists.

0–30 days

Know Before You Choose pilot

Move lender disclosure to the amount/tenure screen for a test cohort; measure recall against the current flow.

Directly targets the documented 4th-screen gap.
31–60 days

Draft the Partner-Exit Playbook

Use the 2024 pause as the worked example; publish in plain language, independent of any current partner-stability concern.

Closes a gap that exists industry-wide, not just at Paytm.
61–90 days

Pilot multi-offer comparison

Test showing 2-3 partner offers side by side for eligible borrowers, where more than one qualified lender exists.

Tests whether visible competition changes the accepted rate.
Ongoing

Track the disconfirming evidence too

Keep monitoring for actual user confusion reports — if the pattern this edition didn't find starts appearing, the priority order should change.

Keeps the research honest as new data arrives.
08 · What we rejected

The RBI news made this tempting to overclaim. We didn't.

Rejected

"Paytm hides who's lending you money."

The lender's name appears on the offer screen, the loan agreement, and the repayment passbook — confirmed against Paytm's own documentation. We found no evidence supporting "hidden."

Not established

"Users are confused about their lender's identity."

A dedicated search for this complaint pattern — reviews, forums, consumer-court filings — found no documented cluster. The defensible claim is narrower: disclosure exists but arrives late, not that it's causing confusion today.

Not supported

"The PPBL licence cancellation threatens your personal loan."

Paytm Payments Bank and the NBFC partners that issue personal loans are separate legal entities. One97's own filings state no financial exposure. We use the PPBL story as an illustration of institutional fragility, not a claim about loan safety.
